Learning outcomes

From lecture theory to strategic decisions

The simulation turns core marketing concepts into decisions with consequences. Students experience how their choices interact with the market and with competing teams.

Segmentation & targeting

Analyse five customer segments, choose target markets and position a brand deliberately.

Marketing mix

Decide on product, price, channels and budget – and weigh the trade-offs between them.

Financial impact

Connect marketing decisions with revenue, cost, production and profit over several years.

Teamwork under competition

Negotiate a shared strategy in the team, respond to competitors and justify decisions.

The market

Five customer segments, one competitive market

Each segment has its own needs and willingness to pay. Teams that choose their target group clearly and serve it consistently gain market share.

Budget

Price-sensitive, low brand loyalty.

Commuters

Comfort and everyday use on the go.

Performance

Sound quality and technology come first.

Lifestyle

Design and brand as a statement.

Eco

Sustainability drives the purchase.

Course formats

Fits the way you teach

You choose the number of company slots and fiscal years per round. Bots take over unfilled slots, so the market stays competitive even with small groups.

Block seminar

An intensive simulation over one or two days, with a debrief after every fiscal year.

Semester course

One fiscal year per session, accompanying the lecture and linking each round to the current topic.

Self-study

Students play alone against bots – ideal as preparation or as an introduction to the model.

How it works

Ready for class in minutes

The process is deliberately simple, so class time goes into discussion and reflection rather than administration.

  1. Create a round

    In the tutor room, set 2–10 company slots and the number of fiscal years.

  2. Teams join

    Students join with name, team name and PIN. Each team is assigned a brand.

  3. Teams decide

    Each team plans product, price, channels, budget and production for the year.

  4. Debrief

    You run the annual calculation; teams compare results and refine their strategy.

FAQ

Questions from lecturers

Do students need an account?

No. Students join with a name, a team name and the PIN you hand out. Nothing needs to be installed.

How many students can take part?

A round has 2 to 10 company slots, and each company can be run by a team of several students. Bots take over slots that are not filled.

Which languages are supported?

The simulation is available in English and German, so it works for international and German-language programmes alike.

Can I try it before using it in class?

Yes. Start a demo against bots right away, or contact us for a pilot round with your course.
